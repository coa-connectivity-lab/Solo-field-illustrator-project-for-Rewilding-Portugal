# Rewilding Dynamics: rabbits, wildcats, pond turtles and a river that stayed

**Status**: started 2026-09-27. Preprint v0.1 deposited on Zenodo, 27 September 2026.

**Preprint**: Angulo Lopez, L. (2026). *Rabbits, wildcats, pond turtles and a river that stayed: a synthesis and research agenda for trophic and hydrological rewilding in the Greater Côa Valley, Portugal*. Preprint v0.1, Zenodo. <https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22997241>

**Author**: Linda Angulo Lopez, ORCID [0009-0009-5689-7148](https://orcid.org/0009-0009-5689-7148)

## Scope

How restoring trophic interactions shapes degraded land and rivers in the Greater Côa Valley, built around three linked species:

1. **European rabbit** (*Oryctolagus cuniculus*): keystone prey in Iberia and an invasive species elsewhere; collapse after myxomatosis, RHD and RHDV2; restocking and habitat drivers of density.
2. **European wildcat** (*Felis silvestris*): the valley's mid-sized predator, its dependence on the rabbit, and mesopredator cascades.
3. **Mediterranean pond turtle** (*Mauremys leprosa*): the river-side animal, an omnivore that depends on summer pools, linked to the rabbit through prey switching.

These sit within four strands of evidence: predators and mesopredators, the rabbit paradox, rewilding and the water cycle, and the anti-dam campaign that left the Côa largely free-flowing. The Eurasian beaver, named as a gap in the companion preprint, is set aside because its native status in Portugal is uncertain.
