# Five storyMaps Ideas


**(i) Three Centuries of a Frontier** — Almeida, 12–14 Aug (local walks, already scheduled)
Opens on the 1766 fortress map, a place built to control terrain and movement, pivots to the same valley managed today for wildlife movement instead. Desk-heavy, minimal new data collection beyond what you already have. Sets up the whole series' framing device, do this one first.

**(ii) Water Without Borders** — Almeida/Côa, 12–14 Aug (same window as i)
Your Pontão Manuel José weir as the small case, the Open Rivers Programme's Ensecadeiras do Côa dam removal as the large one, same barrier theme at two scales. Closes on the verified fish list (*Unio crassus*, *Anguilla anguilla*, *Chondrostoma polylepis*, *Squalius alburnoides*, *Rutilus arcasii*) and the riparian habitat, *Flueggea tinctoria* galleries, willow and alder forest. Feeds the `04_resistance_surface_aquatic.ipynb` work directly, the barrier data is the same data either way.

**(iii) Who Moves Through the Côa** — Merujes do Paulo e André / Paul de Toirões, 15–16 Aug (already scheduled)
The terrestrial target species chapter, Iberian wolf, griffon and Egyptian vulture, roe and red deer, drawn from the verified Endangered Landscapes list rather than the earlier Google leads. This is the one that needs the most care before publishing, cross-check any specific sighting claims against what you actually observe on this trip rather than presenting the ELSP figures as your own field data.

**(iv) 23 Horses, 17 Cattle: Rebuilding the Herd** — Vale Carapito, 19–21 Aug (already scheduled)
Anchors on the Endangered Landscapes numbers, 23 Sorraia horses and 17 Tauros cattle reintroduced, 656 ha under natural grazing. Your own grazer ID and dung beetle field work becomes the ground-truth layer under those headline figures, opens on the numbers, moves to what natural grazing actually looks like up close, closes on the prey-base argument for wolf recovery.

**(v) If the Price Is Biodiversity, Don't Call It Green** — Ribeira do Mosteiro, 24–26 Aug (already scheduled)
Built on Rewilding Portugal's SOPHIA opposition piece, corridor fragmentation as the explicit threat this whole project exists to counter. Frame Ribeira do Mosteiro as part of the same corridor network they describe being cut elsewhere, use their own line about creating new barriers to nature that's already fragmented as the argument, then show what an intact stretch looks like through your own site. Closes the series, brings all four earlier chapters back into one argument about why the corridor matters as a whole rather than as five separate places.

Writing days already blocked at 22–23 Aug and 27–28 Aug cover all five, two days each for the pairs sharing a data source (i+ii, then iv+v), and (iii) gets its own slot given it needs the most source-checking before it's publishable.
