## Habitat description

Maritime pine woodland (*Pinus pinaster*) with an understorey of dry grassland and broom-type shrubs, transitioning to open scrubland (matos) on the more exposed slopes, wind turbines visible on the ridgeline in the distance. This matches the trail description for this stretch, dense pine and scrub replacing the valley's lameiros and chestnut groves once you cross the river and climb toward the border. The whole route is inside the Rede Natura 2000 network, and this exact spot hosts an "Estação de Biodiversidade," a short interpretive trail specifically highlighting butterflies and insects, worth noting since it means there's already baseline biodiversity signage here you could cross-reference.

## Possible bird species

Pine and scrub at this altitude and habitat type on the Iberian border typically holds:

| Species | Notes |
|---|---|
| *Lophophanes cristatus* (Crested tit) | Pine specialist, likely if the stand is mature enough |
| *Loxia curvirostra* (Common crossbill) | Feeds on pine seed cones, listen for it before you see it |
| *Certhia brachydactyla* (Short-toed treecreeper) | Works pine trunks, easy to miss |
| *Curruca undata* (Dartford warbler) | Classic dense-scrub specialist, low and skulking |
| *Lullula arborea* (Woodlark) | Open pine edge/scrub mosaic, song often given in flight |
| *Galerida theklae* (Thekla lark) | Open, rocky, sparse ground cover, matches the granite outcrop terrain here |
| *Saxicola rubicola* (European stonechat) | Perches openly on shrub tops, very visible |
| *Lanius meridionalis* (Iberian grey shrike) | Open scrub with perches, hunts from exposed branches |
| *Circaetus gallicus* (Short-toed eagle) | Soars over open scrub hunting reptiles, plausible overhead sighting |
| *Alectoris rufa* (Red-legged partridge) | Ground-dwelling, dry open slopes |
