## Habitat description

The trail runs through granite-boulder terrain typical of Vale Carapito: a dirt track through open holm-oak/broom scrubland, opening onto rock outcrops draped in extensive pale lichen growth, and a wire-fenced hillside where the fence line cuts directly across a boulder field rather than following a cleared boundary. One frame, taken looking out from under a dead/lichen-hung branch, shows an open grazed clearing with small reddish shapes at the treeline in the middle distance — most likely resting livestock, but too small and distant in the frame to identify confidently. A close photo of fresh dung on the ground (fibrous, dark brown, no visible seed content) is consistent with either horse or cattle, both of which graze this specific trail per the official species panel below — not resolvable to species from the photo alone.

## Species observations — official trail panel (not Linda's own photographs)

**Important:** the species table below comes from Rewilding Portugal's own interpretive panel at the Vale Carapito trailhead ("VALE CARAPITO — Área rewilding do Grande Vale do Côa"), photographed in full by Linda on this visit. The photos of the animals themselves are **not Linda's field photographs** — each one on the panel carries its own photo credit, printed directly beneath the species name. These are Rewilding Portugal's confirmed species list for this specific site, illustrated with photography from named contributors (professional wildlife photographers and, per the credit style, likely also community/citizen-science contributors via eBird/iNaturalist, per the panel's own "Registe as espécies encontradas" callout) — reliable as a site species list, but the individual animal photos on the panel should not be read as Linda's own observations from today.

| # | Functional group | Species name or common name | Photo credit (panel, not Linda's own) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Woodland tree | Pyrenean oak, *Quercus pyrenaica* ("Carvalho-negral") | Juan Carlos Muñoz | Dominant tree of the Vale Carapito forest per the panel; identified by the velvety underside of its leaves |
| 2 | Butterfly | Niobe fritillary, *Fabriciana niobe* ("Borboleta") | Albano Soares | Diurnal, flies May–early June; caterpillars feed on violets and ribwort plantain, overwinter as eggs |
| 3 | Reptile | Algerian psammodromus, *Psammodromus algirus* ("Lagartixa do mato") | HMM Alberto | Panel notes it as probably the easiest reptile to spot at Vale Carapito, well adapted to scrub and forest edges |
| 4 | Amphibian | Iberian tree frog, *Hyla molleri* ("Rela-comum") | André Couto | Found near sunlit water; excellent climber, shelters in tree/rock cavities outside the breeding season |
| 5 | Raptor | Booted eagle, *Hieraaetus pennatus* ("Águia-calçada") | Staffan Widstrand | Migratory, present March–September; nests at Vale Carapito |
| 6 | Bird | Golden oriole, *Oriolus oriolus* ("Papa-figos") | Dietmar Nill | Present April–September; bright yellow, more often heard than seen |
| 7 | Herbivore, ungulate | Roe deer, *Capreolus capreolus* ("Corço") | Tiago Laginha | Solitary, favours forest/clearing mosaics; was locally extinct and is now expanding at this site per the panel |
| 8 | Herbivore, equid (rewilding-introduced) | Sorraia horse, *Equus ferus caballus* ("Cavalo Sorraia") | João Ferreira | Introduced at Vale Carapito to fill the ecological role of the extinct European wild horse; rare Iberian breed with primitive dun/grullo characteristics |

The trail map on the same panel (Percurso VC1) plots roe deer, Sorraia horses, and a raptor as icons near two small water bodies on the loop — a visual confirmation of where the panel expects these species to concentrate, independent of anything Linda photographed directly today.
