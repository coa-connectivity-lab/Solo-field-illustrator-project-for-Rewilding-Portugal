## Habitat description

A managed river beach on the Côa, with wooden platforms, a lifeguard station, and a marked swim area on sand fill. A low weir/dam backs up the channel into the "water mirror" the site is known for locally, this matches the stepped cascade around a small vegetated island photographed here (`wier-island.jpeg`, `island.jpeg`). Above and below the swim area the bank is a dense, multi-stemmed, dark-barked gallery of what reads as common alder (*Alnus glutinosa*) from growth form and leaf shape, with root mats trailing directly into the water and providing cover for small fish. Local descriptions of the beach also mention poplars (*Populus* sp.) giving shade over the picnic area, plausible but not confirmed in any of these photos, the fern-like, pale-flashing foliage backlit in `bird-bathing.jpeg` could be that, or could equally be ash (*Fraxinus angustifolia*) or black locust (*Robinia pseudoacacia*, a common riverside invasive here), the backlighting makes it unreadable. Bramble (*Rubus ulmifolius*) thickets edge the drier ground above the bank, and the slope behind the beach carries dry, lichen-heavy oak woodland, most likely Pyrenean oak (*Quercus pyrenaica*) given the granite hill country, though holm oak (*Q. rotundifolia*) can't be ruled out from these shots. A dry-stone granite wall nearby is old enough to carry heavy crustose lichen and moss cover.
