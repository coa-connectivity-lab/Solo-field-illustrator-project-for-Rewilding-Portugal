## Habitat description

The reservoir sits in a granite-walled valley on the Côa, ringed by rounded, boulder-strewn hills covered in dense, low Mediterranean scrub — the same granite-outcrop terrain the desk study describes for Ermo das Águias generally (`37c4ce09-65df-412d-a142-c8a09d208264.jpeg` shows this hillside close in, bare granite boulders pushing up through matorral). The water itself is broad and still, with a small rocky/granite island near one shore (`3276e2dc-0fe3-4392-b122-938f61bf3aa0.jpeg`) and olive-grove terracing visible on the far slopes in a wider shot (`4bb8335d-fd75-4c34-affc-7eede90d6ea9.jpeg`, which also shows what may be the bridge structure at Ruínas da Ponte de Vale de Madeira in the far distance, not confirmed). A dense stand of rushes/reeds lines the near shore in the foreground of `3276e2dc-0fe3-4392-b122-938f61bf3aa0.jpeg` — likely *Juncus* sp. (cf., not resolvable to species from the photo). Close to the water's edge, a ruined dry-stone building stands beside a cultivated almond tree (*Prunus dulcis*) carrying a hand-painted "VENDE-SE" (for sale) sign (`ca08affc-b3f9-4de6-aedc-e85f34a55775.jpeg`) — consistent with the desk study's land-abandonment narrative for this area (old cereal/olive/grazing land now depopulating). A close-up of dry shrubland (`03b8fdcc-ba58-404f-bf04-e7d6a98da956.jpeg`) shows a sun-bleached, mixed woody shrub cover that may be the white broom (*Cytisus multiflorus*, cf.) the desk study names as now dominant across the wider site, but it's too dried and out of flower in this photo to confirm the species with confidence. A second wide reservoir view (`f1b4772a-c77c-4ee3-9459-d87e0d637695.jpeg`) shows a small white building on the far shore and a distant hilltop structure (possibly a communications mast, unconfirmed) toward what the Maps screenshot labels as Cinco Vilas.

**Important caveat on the dam itself:** none of Linda's own photos show the dam structure — the wall, spillway, or any fish-passage feature. Everything in this note about it being a dam rests on the Google Maps label "Barragem de Vale de Madeira" (visible in the WhatsApp screenshot) plus the visibly still, reservoir-like character of the water body in the photos above. See Open items — this needs direct confirmation on a return visit.

## Open items

- **The dam structure itself needs direct documentation.** Every photo from today shows the reservoir it creates, not the dam wall/spillway/any fish ladder. A return visit specifically to the structure (rather than the shoreline) would let the barrier-permeability assessment above move from "unknown" to an actual finding.
- **Possible conflict with earlier project research.** `deskStudy/movimento-salvaguarda-gravuras-foz-coa.md` concluded the Côa has no completed dam other than the abandoned Foz Côa cofferdam far downstream near the river mouth — this note's "Barragem de Vale de Madeira," much further upstream near Pinhel, appears to contradict that if it's a genuine, completed barrage. Web search today turned up no source that directly confirms a built, named "Barragem de Vale de Madeira": the closest lead is a 2004 Jornal o Interior article ([Autarcas defendem barragem no Alto Côa](https://www.ointerior.pt/arquivo/autarcas-defendem-barragem-no-alto-coa/)) about an EDP-proposed "Alto Côa" hydroelectric scheme in this same stretch of river (Almeida/Pinhel/Vila Nova de Foz Côa/Figueira de Castelo Rodrigo), but that article names different dams (Pêro Martins, Senhora de Monforte) and never mentions Vale de Madeira, and there's no confirmation it was ever built. The Portuguese Wikipedia stub for Vale de Madeira mentions no dam at all. This needs proper desk follow-up before the movimento-salvaguarda note's "mostly free-flowing Côa" framing is either corrected or confirmed to still hold upstream at this reach.
- GPS is approximate only (village/valley-level, not a shoreline fix) — capture an actual Survey123 GPS point at the reservoir on a return visit.
- The Booking.com confirmation PDF and the Google Maps driving-directions PDF found in this same `survey123/ Ermo das Águias/` folder are trip-logistics files, not field content, and are excluded from this note (the Booking.com PDF's GPS line was used only as a coordinate cross-check above).
- The WhatsApp image is a screenshot of the Google Maps app, not one of Linda's own landscape photos — used here only to establish the place names and rough layout (dam upstream, bridge ruins and river beach downstream), not as field observation.
- The broader Ermo das Águias desk study (horses, cattle, cork oak, fire history) remains unconfirmed by field data — today's files don't cover it, so `2026-08-23-Ermo-das-Aguias-Desk-Study.md` still needs its own separate field replacement.
