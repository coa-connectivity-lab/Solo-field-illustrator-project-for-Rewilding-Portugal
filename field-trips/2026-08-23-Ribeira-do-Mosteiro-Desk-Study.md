## Habitat description

North bank of the **Douro River**, municipality of Freixo de Espada à Cinta, inside the Douro International Natural Park. **This is a Douro site, not a Côa River site.** The local watercourse is the **Ribeira do Mosteiro / Mosteiro Stream**, which runs through the valley and enters the Douro. Rewilding Portugal frames the site explicitly as a landscape-connectivity site, which lines up directly with the survey's Connectivity/Movement theme without needing reinterpretation.

**Planned/access GPS (WGS84): 41.041487, -6.908539.** This is a desk-research access-point coordinate, not Linda's field GPS. It should be replaced or supplemented by the actual Survey123 GPS captured during the 23 August visit.

Per desk research: a rugged valley with distinctive geological formations. Old olive and almond groves are recolonising with holm oak, juniper, and terebinth as the rewilding process advances — the original Mediterranean forest composition reasserting itself. The Mosteiro Stream at the valley floor maintains enough humidity to support a notably rich flora against the surrounding dry terrain. The cliffs are described as a refuge for endangered bird species specifically because of their height and inaccessibility — a similar cliff-nesting-raptor framing to Faia Brava, visited earlier this trip (`2026-08-22-Faia-Brava.md`, filed under 23 August pending a date-conflict resolution noted there).

**Cultural layer:** the Calçada de Alpajares is a medieval paved path, possibly dating to the first Portuguese dynasty, historically the main route linking the Miranda lands in the north to the Ribacôa region in the south, in use until early-20th-century road building superseded it — itself a piece of connectivity history, a human movement corridor that predates and parallels the ecological one this survey tracks. At the route's highest point, an old dovecote and medieval rock-cut graves remain visible.
