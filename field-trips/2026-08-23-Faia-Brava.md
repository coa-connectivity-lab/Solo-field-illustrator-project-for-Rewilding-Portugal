## Habitat description

The reserve occupies a deep, rocky river gorge along the Côa: steep granite/schist cliff walls dropping to a narrow valley floor, with the river visible far below carrying a thin ribbon of riparian green through otherwise sparse, sun-bleached scrub and holm-oak cover on the upper slopes. A small road bridge crosses the river at the valley bottom, next to a cluster of buildings, visible from the clifftop viewpoint. Per the on-site "Miradouro de Faia" interpretive panel (photographed in full, transcribed below), this is deliberately poor, thin soil over bare granitic rock, occupied by holm oak copses and tall Spanish broom stands, with more fertile pockets and old olive groves only where the west-facing slopes are gentle enough to hold deeper soil.

**Miradouro de Faia panel, transcribed (Linda's own photo of the panel, not her own field observation):**
"The viewpoint, located near the Cidadelhe village, offers an astonishing view over the Côa river cliffs, one of the most important Douro river leftbank tributaries in the Portuguese territory. In this section, the river flows through a vast granitic area on the bottom of a narrow valley, about 150 to 200 meters below the extensive flattened area where it is lodged. Here, the bare rock outcrops, often covered with yellow lichens, dominate the landscape providing a poor soil which is occupied by copses of holm oaks, and tall formations of Spanish brooms. On the rocky hills of the valley it is possible to observe some exemplars of Montpellier maple (*Acer monspessulanum*), a deciduous tree with a restricted distribution in Portugal, which stands out in the autumn for the intense red colour of its senescent leaves. The west slope, where the hills are smoother, allows the formation of more fertile and deeper soils, being possible to find some ancient olive groves. This place constitutes a privileged spot to observe a rich community of rupicolous birds, which are found in the rocky precipitous hills of the valley. The griffon vulture, a large necrophagous bird, is common here, being its presence guaranteed in any season of the year. In the spring and summer is also possible to observe with some regularity, and from the same spot, species such as the Egyptian vulture and even the rare black stork."
