# Almeida to Porto — Douro Dam Viewpoint, Douro Estuary, Atlantic Beach

Survey123 form: `coa-connectivity-lab-survey` (Greater Côa Valley Multi-Species Connectivity Field Survey — see `survey123/survey123-arcgis-surveys-49049bbd82564ead968d8cc6e90f0de2-design.pdf`)
Source data: `survey123/Porto-esturary -Beach/`
Route sources (Google Maps driving/walking directions, saved PDFs in `field-trips/`, no habitat photos): `2026-08-20-Almedia-Miradouro São Leonardo de Galafura.pdf`, `2026-08-20-Miradouro São Leonardo de Gal -Porto.pdf`, `2026-08-20-Porto Gaia to Nature Reserve Douro Estuary Site, Rio Douro.pdf`, `2026-08-20-Porto Beach.pdf`

This is the first field-trips entry outside the Côa Valley itself — a travel day out to Porto, logged on the same `coa-connectivity-lab-survey` form for consistency with the rest of the record, even though the connectivity-barrier context (a large Douro mainstem dam, an urbanized estuary mouth) is different in kind from the Côa sites.

Per the route PDFs, the day ran Almeida → Miradouro São Leonardo de Galafura (a Douro viewpoint near Peso da Régua, ~181 km/2h20–2h40 from Almeida) → the ibis budget Porto Gaia hotel on R. Martir São Sebastião, Vila Nova de Gaia (~115 km/1h15–1h40 further) → on foot to the Nature Reserve Douro Estuary Site (2.6 km/36 min walk) → a short drive to Praia da Madalena on the Atlantic coast (6.9 km/12–20 min). None of the four route PDFs contain field photos — they're Google Maps direction captures only, used here purely to fix the order of stops and approximate distances/GPS, not as habitat evidence. One additional photo in the source folder, a dusk shot of the Douro mouth and Foz do Douro's lights (`25ed6e92-2fad-48aa-b25b-c82c95c8b9e3.jpeg`), reads as taken from the hotel building itself rather than in the field, so it's used only for scene-setting below and excluded from the species/habitat record.

## Site 1 — Miradouro São Leonardo de Galafura (Douro viewpoint / dam)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| GPS location | N313-1, Galafura, Peso da Régua municipality — a street address from the route PDF, not a captured lat/lon pin |
| Source | Route PDF only (`2026-08-20-Almedia-Miradouro São Leonardo de Galafura.pdf`, `2026-08-20-Miradouro São Leonardo de Gal -Porto.pdf`) — **no photos of this stop exist in the source folder**, so nothing here is from Linda's own field observation |

The Miradouro de São Leonardo de Galafura sits at roughly 566–640 m on a hill east of Galafura, overlooking the Douro River and the terraced Douro Vinhateiro vineyard landscape below — one of the most cited viewpoints in the wine region. The stretch of Douro visible from it runs past Peso da Régua, where the Barragem de Bagaúste (also called the Régua Dam) crosses the river: a 41 m/350 m-long concrete gravity dam with a hydroelectric station and a navigation lock, completed in 1973. It is very likely this dam that was visible from the viewpoint, given the geography, but that's an inference from the route and regional facts, not a confirmed sighting — worth pinning down on the actual photos or memory of the stop if that matters for the record.

## Site 2 — Douro Estuary Nature Reserve (Reserva Natural Local do Estuário do Douro)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| GPS location | South bank of the Douro mouth, Cabedelo do Douro sandbar/dune restinga, near Av. Deoceliano Monteiro, Vila Nova de Gaia — approximate, read from the pin location in the walking-directions PDF; exact lat/lon not captured |
| Source | `WhatsApp Image 2026-08-20 at 18.46.42.jpeg`, `c5b56891-b97e-42b0-a4b7-cf124a6291c2.jpeg`, `71059b80-bb99-4324-b179-e3fd5673c6dc.jpeg` — all Linda's own photos |

The reserve is a 62-hectare protected area on the estuary's south bank, combining a coastal dune restinga (Cabedelo), open sand/mudflat, and marsh, with boardwalk access and two birdwatching observatories. The photos here match that description closely: dry, cured dune grassland in the foreground (one photo shows a sandy footpath cutting through it), opening onto a broad tidal sandbar and channel with the Foz do Douro/Gaia waterfront skyline behind. Water level and exposed sand vary between the three photos, consistent with tidal movement over the course of the walk rather than different sites.

Two of the three photos show groups of pale, long-legged wading birds standing and foraging in the shallow water, plus a much larger, denser flock (reads as gulls, unconfirmed to species) massed on the sandbar in the background of the WhatsApp image and `c5b56891`. The wading birds aren't resolvable to species from these images — see Species observations below for the confidence flag and reasoning.

## Site 3 — Praia da Madalena (Atlantic coast)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| GPS location | R. do Mar 450, 4405-760 Madalena, Vila Nova de Gaia (postal address from the route PDF, not a lat/lon) |
| Source | `2f337aaa-a547-4b1f-a0aa-42a1fdad7c4d.jpeg`, `593d2c8a-8c2d-4fd5-8377-313f046f6c1a.jpeg`, `41b864f3-67ef-41b2-a3d0-ab96ed7b6c32.jpeg`, `8f3688cf-b203-4c45-864a-f499725c38dc.jpeg` — all Linda's own photos |

A rocky Atlantic beach: large, rounded, weathered granite boulders scattered across a narrow sand strip, with the open Atlantic breaking over rockier ground further out. The boulders carry orange crustose lichen and are colonized in their cracks by classic Iberian coastal-rock flora — sea fennel (*Crithmum maritimum*, cf.) and the invasive iceplant *Carpobrotus* sp. (see Species observations), both rooted straight into rock crevices with almost no soil. A close-up of the sand turned up the remains of a crab (cf., unconfirmed to species — see below), legs and claws still articulated but empty/dried, likely a predation remnant or a molt rather than a fresh mortality.

## Open items

- Dam identity at Site 1 (Barragem de Bagaúste) is inferred from the viewpoint's known location relative to Peso da Régua, not confirmed by a photo or on-site note — worth checking against memory of the actual view, or a photo if one turns up elsewhere.
- Wading-bird ID at the Douro Estuary is unresolved between flamingo and little egret (or another species); the 2010 flamingo record for this same reserve makes it plausible but not confirmed. Worth zooming into the original photos at full resolution, since the birds are small in frame here.
- Gull flock on the sandbar is not resolved past "likely one or more of the reserve's three common gull species."
- Order/timing of the three Douro Estuary photos isn't confirmed — lighting varies between them (bright/glare vs. flatter overcast), which may reflect changing conditions during the ~36-minute walk rather than separate visits, but this isn't confirmed.
- The dusk photo from the hotel (`25ed6e92-2fad-48aa-b25b-c82c95c8b9e3.jpeg`) was excluded from the habitat/species record as not being a field-visit photo; flag if it should be logged separately as accommodation context.
