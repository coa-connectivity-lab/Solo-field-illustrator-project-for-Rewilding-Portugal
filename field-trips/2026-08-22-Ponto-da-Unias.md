## Habitat description

The Côa here runs through a steep, rocky gorge: bare granite/schist walls and boulder-strewn banks on both sides, with a moderate cover of low scrub and scattered trees on the upper slopes where soil has accumulated, thinning to bare rock closer to the water. The river itself is slow and pooled at this point — dark, still water reflecting the gorge walls, rather than fast-moving or rapids. This is classic Côa river-gorge terrain, the same rock-walled river-corridor character documented at other Côa sites (Coa-Gravures-Penascosa, Coa-vs-Douro-Rivers).

The memorial plaque reads (partial transcription, weathered stone): "PONTE DA UNIÃO — Esta placa é oferecida em memória aos fundadores da ponte suspensa, aos que trabalharam com heroísmo pelo futuro da nossa terra, que fizeram dela um grande centro transitável. Albano José Matias e José Joaquim Guerra... porque o feroz rio Côa derrubou os primeiros pegões e eles não desanimaram, reconstruindo outros novamente... Mais tarde fez-se o rompimento da estrada pelo povo e Junta de Freguesia... Mais tarde o tabuleiro da ponte em cimento..." — dated 1995. In summary: the plaque commemorates the original founders of a suspension bridge here, who rebuilt it after the Côa's floods repeatedly knocked down the first supports, and records the later work of opening the road and casting the bridge deck in concrete. It's a direct, first-person record of the river as a historical barrier to be engineered around — exactly the kind of human-connectivity-infrastructure history this survey is tracking, on the human side of the same corridor the wildlife barriers document.
