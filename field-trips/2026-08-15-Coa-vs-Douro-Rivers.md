## Habitat description

**Côa side** (`5dd11096-bffb-4baa-84ec-b4dd30b0cec2.jpeg`, `61156faa-04c8-4293-8d24-01ddd72fd7a6.jpeg`): a steep schist gorge, near-vertical cliff faces cut into sheet-like slabs (the same rock type that carries the Côa's rock art), with a small, narrow, dark water body just visible at the bottom of frame, in scale with the Côa rather than the Douro. Slopes are covered in dense Mediterranean matorral scrub, with an old dry-stone terrace wall and a small ruined stone structure (function unclear from these angles, possibly a field shelter or a dovecote) being reclaimed by scrub, evidence of abandoned terracing rather than active agriculture.

**Douro side** (`c0ec90e1-9b3a-415a-acd4-d892d2cc9e3f.jpeg`, `f2ce5fe3-5a31-4114-8dd6-72ad088d5f90.jpeg`): a much wider valley, a broad, glassy, mirror-still river filling the gorge floor, terraced Douro Superior vineyards stepping right down to the waterline on the near bank, with drier scrub-and-holm-oak slopes above the terraces and across the water. The stillness of the water here isn't natural low flow, it's the Barragem do Pocinho reservoir: the dam sits on the Douro in this same municipality, between the Côa and Sabor confluences, and its reservoir backs water up for roughly 40 km, comfortably enough to reach and drown out any natural current at these viewpoints.

## Comparison

| | Côa (gorge) | Douro Superior (this stretch) |
|---|---|---|
| Channel | Narrow, schist gorge, small dark pools | Wide, reservoir-flooded valley |
| Flow character | Natural, free-flowing (see barrier note below) | Regulated/still, backed up by the Pocinho reservoir |
| Bank vegetation | Dense evergreen matorral scrub on near-vertical cliff | Terraced vineyard to the waterline; scrub/holm oak on steeper untillable ground |
| Land use at the water's edge | Minimal, abandoned terracing reclaimed by scrub | Active, intensive viticulture (Douro Superior wine landscape) |
| Structures | One small ruined stone structure, abandoned | None visible in these frames |

## Possible/expected species (not observed, habitat-based)

The Côa/Douro Superior confluence zone sits at the edge of the wider Douro Internacional cliff-and-gorge system, well known for cliff-nesting raptors and large soaring birds:

| Species | Notes |
|---|---|
| *Gyps fulvus* (Griffon vulture) | Colonial cliff-nester, classic species of the Douro Internacional/Douro Superior schist gorges |
| *Neophron percnopterus* (Egyptian vulture) | Summer visitor to the same cliff systems, smaller and more solitary than the griffon |
| *Aquila fasciata* (Bonelli's eagle) | Resident cliff-nesting raptor of these gorges, hunts over the scrub slopes |
| *Ciconia nigra* (Black stork) | Nests on cliff ledges along the Douro Internacional/Superior, forages in slower river stretches such as the Pocinho reservoir |
| *Circaetus gallicus* (Short-toed eagle) | Open scrub/matorral hunter, plausible over either slope |

## Page 3 — Connectivity barriers

| Barrier | Type | Permeability |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Barragem do Pocinho, Douro mainstem, Vila Nova de Foz Côa (between the Côa and Sabor confluences) | Large hydroelectric dam, completed 1982/99 | Fully blocking for fish and aquatic connectivity along tens of kilometres of the Douro; its reservoir is what makes the Douro read as still water in these photos, not natural low flow |
| 2 | The Côa itself, by contrast | No completed dam — a separate hydroelectric project planned directly on the Côa (not Pocinho, a different structure on a different river) was cancelled in January 1996 after a two-year civic and parliamentary movement, specifically to protect the rock art. Full history in `deskStudy/movimento-salvaguarda-gravuras-foz-coa.md` | Mostly free-flowing, though not entirely: an abandoned cofferdam from that cancelled project was never demolished and still periodically floods panels upstream (see the desk study note). The clearest connectivity contrast in this pair of sites is still that the Côa tributary is largely intact where the Douro mainstem it feeds into is permanently and fully regulated |
| 3 | Abandoned terracing/dry-stone walls on the Côa gorge slope | Old agricultural land-use edge, now being reclaimed by scrub | Never a hard barrier to wildlife, and progressively less of one as the terraces revert |
