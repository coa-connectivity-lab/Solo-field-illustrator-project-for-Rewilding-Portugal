Whose "safe and just"? Indigenous Science, Earth system justice, and a correction to this project's own framing
Linda Angulo Lopez, compiled 23 August 2026, desk research correcting the "safe and just" framing used since v3 of `research/eco-connectivity/notebook/coa_eco_connectivity_story_v4.ipynb`

This note exists because a citation in this project's own work was wrong, in two rounds. v3 and v4 of the eco-connectivity notebook credited "safe and just" to Kate Raworth's 2012 "doughnut economics" model, a biophysical planetary-boundaries ring plus a social-foundation ring. That framing is corrected here, twice over: (i) "safe and just" is fundamentally about justice, equity, diversity and inclusion, not a biophysical boundary with a welfare footnote attached, and (ii) the *origin* of that idea, living within ecological limits while upholding justice and right relationship with the land, is not a 2012 Oxfam paper at all. It comes from Indigenous Science and community-led climate justice movements, which have held this idea for far longer than any Western academic framework.

## Indigenous Science as the origin, not a footnote

**Jessica Hernandez, Ph.D.** (Binnizá/Zapotec and Maya Ch'orti'), Indigenous environmental scientist, founder of the environmental agency Piña Soul, and Director of Indigenous Science & Research at Earth Daughters, makes exactly this argument in *Fresh Banana Leaves: Healing Indigenous Landscapes Through Indigenous Science* (North Atlantic Books, 2022; a 2022 Los Angeles Times Book Prize finalist in Science & Technology). Her core claim, documented in interviews around the book, is that Indigenous communities are among those most affected by climate change, yet Indigenous science, holistic land, water and forest management built from millennia of accumulated knowledge, is routinely excluded, otherized, or treated as "soft" in mainstream environmental policy and discourse ([WBUR/Here & Now, "'Fresh Banana Leaves' spotlights Indigenous science for a warming planet"](https://www.wbur.org/hereandnow/2022/04/28/indigenous-science-climate); [Audubon, "Jessica Hernandez on Why Western Environmentalism Won't Save Us"](https://www.audubon.org/magazine/jessica-hernandez-why-western-environmentalism-wont-save-us)). Her own site ([jessicabhernandez.com](https://www.jessicabhernandez.com/fresh-banana-leaves.html)) and [Wikipedia entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Hernandez) confirm this as an active, widely credentialed body of work, not a fringe position.

This project now treats that argument as the correct starting point for "safe and just": the idea that a landscape can be lived with, not just extracted from, without giving up on justice for the people and other species already there, is older and more widely held than any single 2009, 2012, or 2023 paper. Kate Raworth is not cited anywhere in this project going forward.

## Gupta and the Earth Commission: the academic articulation, downstream of that idea, not its source

Where this project's framework does draw on a formal academic model is the Earth Commission's 2023 work, and specifically **Joyeeta Gupta**, Professor of Environment and Development in the Global South, University of Amsterdam, and co-chair of the Earth Commission. Two papers matter here:

(i) Rockström et al. 2023, "[Safe and just Earth system boundaries](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06083-8)," *Nature* 619:102-111, which quantifies safe and just Earth System Boundaries (ESBs) for climate, biosphere integrity, water, nutrient cycles, and aerosols, at global and sub-global scales, and finds that seven of eight globally quantified boundaries are already exceeded.

(ii) Gupta et al. 2023, "[Earth system justice needed to identify and live within Earth system boundaries](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-023-01064-1)," *Nature Sustainability*, Gupta's own paper defining Earth system justice across four dimensions, interspecies, intergenerational, international, and intragenerational, and across two forms, substantive and procedural justice.

Per the Earth Commission's own account, "[Safe and Just Earth System Boundaries](https://earthcommission.org/safe-and-just-earth-system-boundaries/)" and its release statement "[To ensure a safe and just future... boundaries must include justice](https://earthcommission.org/to-ensure-a-safe-and-just-future-for-people-nature-and-the-planet-earth-system-boundaries-must-include-justice-researchers-find/)," Gupta specifically led the introduction of justice into what had, until 2023, been a purely biophysical planetary-boundaries framework (the original Rockström et al. 2009 boundaries, discussed in the companion note `safe-and-just-social-foundations-planetary-boundaries.md`). A [Mongabay interview, "Joyeeta Gupta: The Indian voice for environmental justice at global platforms"](https://india.mongabay.com/2023/07/interview-joyeeta-gupta-the-indian-voice-for-environmental-justice-at-global-platforms/), establishes her own Global South positioning directly. Linda writes from that same position (South African/French national, see the `my-cv` skill), which is part of why this correction matters to her personally, not only academically.

## The Earth Commission's own framework is not beyond critique either

Holding Gupta's work as the formal articulation this project cites does not mean treating it as beyond question. A real, citable epistemic-justice critique of the planetary-boundaries lineage exists: the framework "originated with a self-selected group of scientists deciding what the 'environmental safe space for humanity' was, without any input from 'humanity'." The [Lancet Planetary Health-Earth Commission report](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanplh/article/PIIS2542-5196(24)00042-1/fulltext) on "translations and transformations," and separate work on [epistemicide and planetary health](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11361672/), both discuss re-centering Indigenous knowledge systems against exactly this critique. So the honest structure here is not "Hernandez cited by Gupta," a direct link that does not appear to exist in the literature and should not be asserted, but two legitimate, differently positioned voices in the same intellectual space: Indigenous Science holding the underlying idea long before it had an Earth-system-science name, and the Earth Commission's 2023 work as a genuine, still-imperfect, still-Western-academy-housed step toward building justice into biophysical science.

## What changes in this project

Every place this project has used "safe and just," in the eco-connectivity notebook's framing section, its eco-tourism discussion, and its Limpopo comparison, gets corrected to this lineage: Indigenous Science and community-led climate justice movements as the origin, Gupta and the Earth Commission as the academic articulation this project draws its quantified language from, and no citation of Raworth anywhere.
