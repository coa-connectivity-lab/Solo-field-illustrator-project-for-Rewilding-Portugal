Río Côa desk study notes — weir/battlefield section
Linda Angulo Lopez, 11 August 2026

Site context

The Côa rises in the Sierra de las Mesas, in the freguesia of Fóios, right on the Spanish border, and runs 135 km through a deep valley before joining the Douro near Vila Nova de Foz Côa. The valley's depth explains the static position of the border here on its own. Its Palaeolithic rock-art sites earned it World Heritage status.

Site name: Pontão Manuel José (Martins), Río Côa, Almeida. Plus Code M3HH+FC, Almeida.

GPS location: 40.6787318, -6.9213772.

Access: 6.6 km, 8 min by car from Almeida. Head southeast on Av. da Fortaleza/N332, turn right onto Av. de São Pedro/N340 and follow it for 3.1 km, turn left for 2.2 km, turn right for 1.2 km, then turn right for the final 67 m, destination on the left.

The Pontão Manuel José is a footbridge/weir crossing about 2.5 km upstream from the road bridge below Almeida, on the GR45/Grande Rota do Vale do Côa, linking Cinco Vilas on the right bank to Vale de Madeira on the left. It replaced an older stepping-stone ford (pontão de poldras), used when the Côa ran lower before downstream dam construction raised the water level over it. This matches the low azud/mill weir photographed at the site: (i) the low structure crossing the river in the photograph is this pontão/açude itself, and (ii) a higher hydroelectric dam further downstream backs up the reservoir that now partly submerges it. The site sits within the historic Côa battlefield, close to the Ponte Grande crossed by four thousand men during the retreat before the pursuing force of ten thousand.

Species recorded

(i) Apis mellifera, confirmed as a European honey bee, foraging along the bank.

(ii) Anax imperator, an Emperor Dragonfly, light green body consistent with a female or immature male. Anax parthenope remains the alternative if the eyes read blue-green rather than the deeper blue typical of A. imperator. Both are among the commonest large dragonflies on slow rivers and reservoir edges in Portugal.

(iii) Pieris brassicae or P. rapae, the two "cabbage white" species most likely on disturbed, open ground near the river.

(iv) Squalius carolitertii and Pseudochondrostoma duriense, the two small-bodied cyprinids endemic to the Douro basin, the most plausible identity for the tiny fish observed in the pool.

(v) Dolomedes fimbriatus, the raft/fishing spider genuinely present in Iberia. True Argyroneta aquatica doesn't occur this far south, so if the animal was skating on the surface rather than resting on rocks, it was more likely a water strider (Gerris sp.), an insect, not a spider.

(vi) Cattle (Bos taurus), free-ranging on hillside pasture, photographed grazing under pine and oak above the N340 near the Rua Doutor Chegão junction, Almeida. Breed unconfirmed, possibly Maronesa or Mirandesa given the region, but this is a guess. This is the traditional extensive grazing pattern for the arid hill pasture around Almeida, unfenced or lightly fenced, with a stock fence running along the road edge separating grazing land from the N340.

(vii) Dung beetles on the grazed plots: Copris hispanus, Copris lunaris, Bubas bubalus, Onthophagus vacca and O. opacicollis dominate cattle and horse dung on Mediterranean holm-oak pasture in Iberia. Scarabaeus laticollis is worth adding only if the beetle was rolling a ball rather than tunnelling straight down.

Barriers logged

(i) The azud, low head, partially crossable for stronger swimmers, fully blocking for weaker ones.

(ii) The hydroelectric dam, fully blocking unless it has a fish pass, not photographed but referenced in the original site description.

(iii) The road and stock fence above the site, partial barrier for medium and large mammals depending on fence height, and a standing mortality risk for smaller fauna crossing the tarmac.
