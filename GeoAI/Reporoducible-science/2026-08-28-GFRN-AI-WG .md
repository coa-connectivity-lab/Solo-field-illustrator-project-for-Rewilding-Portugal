## Why the group exists

The International Science Council (ISC) has launched a Global Reporting Standard for
AI disclosure, with COPE, the STM publishers association and the Global Young Academy
as coordinating partners. Consultation runs in three rounds: round 1 (December 2025 to
February 2026), round 2 (now, feedback due October 2026) and a final round. The
working group was formed to give GFRN one consensus response and, specifically, to
add the reproducibility angle the ISC draft currently lacks. The conversation
predates the ISC launch: the UK, French and Swiss RNs were already discussing an
AI-use taxonomy earlier this year.

## What the standard proposes

(i) Disclose any AI use in the research lifecycle that influences results,
interpretation or reporting. (ii) It covers all AI agents and tools, not LLMs alone.
(iii) It applies only to peer-reviewed articles. Round 1 set six target qualities:
easy to complete, structured, machine-readable, flexible in description, able to name
different tools, and adaptable across disciplines.

## Round 2 open questions

(i) Which AI use crosses the threshold for disclosure. (ii) Where disclosure sits,
inside the relevant sections or as a separate statement. (iii) The taxonomy: what to
report and how, consistently across disciplines. (iv) Whether declaring no AI use
should be mandatory. (v) How to signal responsibility and accountability for who did
what.

## Points most relevant to Software Heritage

- **No agreed way to measure AI contribution.** Two participants (Olga, Oleksandr)
  already use local Git-based methods to count human decisions against agent-written
  lines of code. Olga noted that under Swiss rules authorship protection is lost once
  AI produces more than roughly half the output, so a measure matters legally, not
  only editorially.
- **Reproducibility and authorship are different questions.** For reproducibility the
  provenance of a ten-line snippet barely matters; for assessing a person's work it
  matters a great deal. The group wants the taxonomy to keep the two separate.
- **Provenance over detection.** Detection tools are unreliable and disclosure leans
  on researcher honesty, so a version-controlled, archived history is a firmer basis.
  This is close to Software Heritage's remit.
- **Bias risk.** A blunt "AI used" tag invites stigma, and participants flagged this
  as heavier for Global South researchers, which is the argument for a threshold and
  for granular, activity-level reporting.
- **Model to build on.** The draft taxonomy has 18 activity points (idea generation,
  literature search, data processing, drafting and so on) and is explicitly modelled
  on CRediT.

taxonomy that GFRN can offer as an annex to the ISC standard. Findings go to the

## Topic lead: which to volunteer for

The Round 2 workplan asks for one or two leads per consultation topic, plus a
cross-cutting review of existing AI reporting standards and literature. The lead
role is coordination and synthesis, not writing the section alone: pull together
member comments, examples and literature, mark agreement, disagreement and open
questions, draft the GFRN recommendations for the topic, then work with the other
leads on the consolidated response. Steps run sign-up, contribute, synthesise,
consolidate, review, submit, with an optional extension into a position or
research paper and a reproducibility-oriented reporting framework or taxonomy.
