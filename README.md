**[Solo field illustrator project for Rewilding Portugal](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1wNyYlElJhagsg_7NNhoL7V2yz-BWiEKrkB091rVS1sM/edit?usp=sharing)**

## Project Overview

This repository contains the planning, field studies, sketches, ecological notes, and illustration development for a natural history illustration project in the Greater Côa Valley, Portugal.

The project explores how rewilding restores ecological processes through three connected landscape stories:

1. **Movement** — herbivores restoring ecological dynamics  
2. **Water** — wetlands recovering after human disturbance  
3. **Connection** — landscapes allowing ecosystems to function together  

The final outcome will be a triptych of field-study landscape plates supported by species sketches, habitat studies, and ecological observations.

# Global Artivism Month 2026

**Update, 27 September 2026.** The triptych is now the coa-connectivity-lab contribution to [Global Artivism Month](https://globalartivism.ca) (1 November to 10 December 2026, closing on Human Rights Day). Global Artivism is a Global South and Global Majority-led movement of artists and activists creating change through culture.

Live gallery, in English, Portuguese, Spanish and French: <https://coa-connectivity-lab.github.io/global-artivism/> (direct link to a language with `?lang=pt`, `?lang=es` or `?lang=fr`).

The gallery, *Movement, Water, Connection*, pairs each field illustration with a connectivity map from the [eco-connectivity workflow](https://github.com/coa-connectivity-lab/eco-connectivity-workflow):

| Panel | Illustration | Map |
|---|---|---|
| i. Movement | Herbivores at Vale Carapito | Land connectivity (wolf, wildcat, red deer), fire-aware |
| ii. Water | Paul de Toirões wetland | Connectivity along rivers and streams, catchment-aware |
| iii. Connection | Côa gorge and Penascosa rock art | Multispecies connectivity, strongest along the lower Côa to the Douro |

What has changed since August:

(i) The three maps are rendered and live. The illustrations will be published during the month.

(ii) An interactive map shows the 20 field sites visited in August 2026, from Fóios to Faia Brava. Species records are not shown, to protect sensitive animals.

(iii) A new series is in preparation: the European rabbit, the European wildcat and the Eurasian beaver, drawn as new works after the Iron Age fine-line engravings of the Côa Valley. None of the three is known in the valley's rock art. Drawing them in its graphic language asks which animals belong in the next chapter of this landscape.

(iv) The methods are written up as a preprint for Zenodo, *Movement, Water, Connection: a reproducible Python connectivity workflow and field illustration for the Greater Côa Valley rewilding landscape*, with a second preprint on rabbits, predators and river hydrology in draft.

## Join the Côa call: collaborations welcome

The gallery is also an open call. Illustrators, field-notebook keepers, ecologists, farmers, hunters, students, and anyone who lives near the Côa, or cares about a river of their own, are invited to take part. No experience and no travel needed.

Three ways to join:

(i) **Witness.** Follow the month and share the posts.

(ii) **Contribute one page.** One place, one species, one sentence. A photo of a paper page is enough.

(iii) **Co-create.** Help build a shared map and zine of the Côa corridor.

Collaborations beyond the call are also welcome: rabbit and wildcat survey data, beaver habitat expertise, Portuguese and Spanish translation review, and connectivity modelling in Python.
