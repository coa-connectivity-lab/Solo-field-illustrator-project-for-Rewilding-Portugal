# Cao Valley Observation Plan

## Daily Observation Practice

### Local Landscape Study Base — Almeida

During the residency, Almeida functions as both accommodation base and accessible field laboratory. Its position within the Greater Côa Valley allows repeated observation of the surrounding landscape without the logistical demands of travelling to the principal project locations.

Short local walks provide a continuous opportunity to develop visual familiarity with the region and establish a reference framework for the three main illustration narratives.

These repeated observations support:

* developing familiarity with the Greater Côa landscape
* studying changing light, weather, and seasonal conditions
* observing common species and ecological processes
* practising rapid landscape composition
* collecting visual references between major field visits
* understanding relationships between river systems, agriculture, woodland, and human history


## Primary Local Observation Route

### Almeida → Ponte Grande sobre o Rio Côa → Riverside GR Trail → Almeida

**Distance:** 5–7 km
**Duration:** approximately 1.5–2 hours

This circular route descends from Almeida’s fortress towards the historic bridge over the River Côa, follows a section of the Grande Rota do Côa, and returns towards the town.

The route provides a practical study area for understanding how natural and cultural landscapes interact.

## Key Observation Themes

### River and Ecological Systems

* river corridor structure
* riparian vegetation
* water movement and seasonal conditions
* habitat connectivity along the Côa Valley

### Landscape Structure

* granite geology
* oak woodland regeneration
* agricultural terraces
* vegetation transitions
* relationships between cultivated and recovering landscapes

### Cultural Landscape

* Almeida fortress and valley relationship
* historical land management patterns
* connections between human settlement and ecological systems

The local study area provides opportunities for:

* panoramic studies of Almeida overlooking the valley

## Ecological Role Within the Project

Although Almeida–Rio Côa is not one of the three principal illustration sites, it provides an essential contextual landscape for understanding ecological connectivity across the Greater Côa Valley.

Repeated observations of this accessible corridor reveal relationships between:

* river systems
* agricultural heritage
* woodland regeneration
* geological structure
* human settlement
* ecological recovery

The Almeida study area acts as a continuous reference point throughout the residency, supporting the development of the three main narratives:

**Movement — Vale Carapito**
Animal movement, grazing systems, woodland/meadow relationships, and seasonal landscape change.

**Water — Paul de Toirões**
Wetland restoration, aquatic systems, habitat recovery, and ecological return.

**Connection — Ribeira do Mosteiro**
Valley structure, geological relationships, Mediterranean vegetation, and links between habitats and human history.

The local landscape provides a flexible field option during remote-work days and when longer excursions are limited by heat, access conditions, or energy levels.
