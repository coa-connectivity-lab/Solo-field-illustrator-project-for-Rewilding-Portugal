# Daily Observation Practice

## Local Landscape Study Base — Almeida

During the residency, Almeida serves as both accommodation and an accessible field laboratory. Short walks from the town provide opportunities for repeated observation without the logistical demands of travelling to the primary project sites.

These walks support:

* developing visual familiarity with the Greater Côa landscape
* studying changing light throughout the day
* observing common species and ecological processes
* practising rapid landscape composition
* collecting reference material between major field visits

### Primary Local Route

**Almeida → Ponte Grande sobre o Rio Côa → Riverside GR trail → Almeida**

**Distance:** 5–7 km
**Duration:** 1.5–2 hours

This circular walk follows the descent from Almeida's fortress to the historic bridge over the River Côa before joining a section of the Grande Rota do Côa and returning to the fortress.

Key observation themes:

* river corridor structure
* granite geology
* riparian vegetation
* oak woodland regeneration
* agricultural terraces
* fortress–landscape relationship
* evidence of ecological connectivity along the Côa Valley

Illustration opportunities:

* panoramic studies of Almeida overlooking the valley
* bridge perspective studies
* riverbank vegetation
* cloud and light studies
* quick colour notes
* compositional thumbnails

## Daily Sketch Routine

When not visiting priority project locations:

**Morning (06:00–08:00)**

* 20-minute landscape thumbnail
* light direction studies
* habitat notes

**Evening (19:30–21:30)**

* colour studies
* atmosphere
* river observations
* species behaviour
* photographic reference

These repeated visits create a visual baseline that supports the three principal illustration plates.

## Ecological Role Within the Project

Although not one of the three principal illustration sites, the Almeida–Rio Côa corridor provides an important contextual landscape for understanding ecological connectivity across the Greater Côa Valley. Repeated observations of this accessible section help establish the visual language of the project, revealing how rivers, agricultural heritage, woodland regeneration, and human history interact within a recovering landscape.

This local study area acts as a consistent reference point throughout the residency, complementing the more site-specific narratives of **Movement** (Vale Carapito), **Water** (Paul de Toirões), and **Connection** (Ribeira do Mosteiro). It also gives you a low-effort field option on remote-work days or when conditions (heat, access, or fatigue) make longer excursions impractical.
