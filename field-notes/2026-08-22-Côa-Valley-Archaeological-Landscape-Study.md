# Full-Day Field Exercise — Côa Valley Archaeological Landscape Study

**Date:** 22 August 2026

**Theme:**
**Human–Landscape Connection — Archaeology, Animals, and Place**

**Location:**
Côa Valley archaeological landscape

**Purpose:**

A cultural landscape study exploring the relationship between prehistoric human activity, animals, geology, and the wider Côa Valley environment.

The objective is to understand how humans have observed, represented, and interacted with this landscape over thousands of years.

This visit complements the ecological themes of the residency by adding a historical perspective on landscape connection.

---

# Morning — Archaeological Landscape Orientation

Visit the archaeological site and surrounding landscape.

Focus on:

* relationship between rock art locations and terrain
* valley structure
* geological formations
* river corridor connections
* visibility and viewpoints across the landscape

Record:

* landscape orientation
* rock surfaces and textures
* terrain forms
* relationship between cliffs, water, and movement routes

Create:

* landscape thumbnails
* geological sketches
* viewpoint studies
* visual notes on human interaction with place

# Midday — Rock Art and Species Observation

Study the prehistoric representations and their ecological context.

Focus on:

* animal forms represented in the engravings
* relationships between animals and landscape
* movement and behaviour suggested through imagery
* connections between past and present ecosystems

Supporting sketches:

* animal movement studies
* simplified form studies
* rock surface textures
* landscape silhouettes

Questions:

* What animals shaped human perception of this landscape?
* How does ancient imagery reveal ecological relationships?
* How can past and present landscapes be connected visually?
